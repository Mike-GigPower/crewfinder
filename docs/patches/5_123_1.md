# 5.123.1

*Released 25 November 2026*

The SmartStaff graph on the System tab is now bars, one per minute, and the minutes behind it stop changing.

The old graph drew a line through the minutes. With one person using THE GOAT that was the wrong shape for the data: most minutes have no requests at all, so the line fell into short disconnected pieces, and a busy minute with quiet minutes on both sides had nothing to join to and so was drawn as nothing at all — the very minute you most wanted to see. The scale ran up to the worst minute of the hour, so a single twelve-second spike pressed every normal minute flat along the bottom.

Now each minute that had requests is a bar of its own. Its height is the typical time that minute, coloured green, amber or red against the same cut-offs the service dots use, and the thin tick above it is that minute's slowest request. A quiet minute is simply blank, so a lone busy minute stands up on its own.

The scale is fixed at eight seconds and no longer follows the peak, so normal minutes stay readable on a bad day. Anything taller is cut off at the top and labelled with its real value, so you still see that a minute hit twelve seconds without losing the shape of everything else. Down the left-hand side the two faint lines are now labelled with the numbers they mark, and the times along the bottom are filled in at each quarter hour, so you can tell when something happened without counting slots.

Hovering anywhere in a minute's column now gives its readout, instead of only the line itself, so a fast minute is as easy to read as a slow one. A quiet minute says so.

**The graph was also quietly rewriting its own history.** Minutes that had already finished could change from one refresh to the next, which is why the earlier version looked different every time you glanced at it: the sixty slots were measured backwards from the current instant rather than fixed to the clock, so every five-second refresh slid all sixty edges along and requests moved between minutes. A request made at 15:44:30 was reported under 15:45 at one moment and under 15:44 half a minute later. The minutes are now pinned to the clock, so once a minute has finished it is final, and the only column that still moves is the one still filling.
